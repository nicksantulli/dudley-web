Privacy Policy

Table Talk: Conversation Cards · Effective September 15, 2026

The short version

  • Table Talk is a free conversation-card app, paid for by ads and optional in-app purchases. There are no accounts. Every card is bundled in the app, and your bookmarks stay on your device.
  • When you first open the app, Apple asks two questions: may the app track you, and may it send notifications. Tapping “Allow” on tracking allows personalized ads and turns on anonymous usage analytics and crash reports. Otherwise ads are non-personalized, and analytics and crash reports stay off unless you turn them on.
  • No ads at all in the EEA, the UK or Switzerland.
  • We don't sell your data for money. Some US state laws count personalized ads as “selling” or “sharing”, and you can opt out at any time by turning tracking off.

What changed on September 15, 2026

  • Ads can now be personalized, but only for people who tap “Allow” on Apple's tracking prompt. Everyone else gets non-personalized ads.
  • Besides Google AdMob, ads can now come from Meta Audience Network. Other partner networks listed below may be added later.
  • This policy now states that there are no ads in the EEA, the UK or Switzerland, and that ads are non-personalized when the app can't read your region.
  • New section on US state privacy rights (California, Virginia, Colorado, Connecticut, Utah, Texas, Oregon and others), including how to opt out.
  • The earlier version was out of date. This one now covers usage analytics (PostHog), crash reports (Sentry), the weekly reminder and in-app purchases.

Versions of the app released before this date request non-personalized ads only.

Your choices at first launch

  • Tracking (Apple's prompt). “Allow” allows personalized ads and turns on usage analytics and crash reports. “Ask App Not to Track” keeps ads non-personalized and leaves analytics and crash reports off.
  • Notifications (Apple's prompt). “Allow” turns on one optional weekly reminder. “Don't Allow” leaves it off.

You can change all of this later. Table Talk's Settings → Privacy has switches for Usage analytics and Crash reports, and Settings → Reminders controls the reminder. To change tracking, see Advertising below.

What stays on your device

  • Your bookmarks, your “Shuffle My Picks” deck and your recent card history.
  • Your settings, reminder schedule, and purchase and ads state.

We don't receive any of this. Deleting the app removes it from your device.

Prompts and sharing

All prompt text is included in the app. When you share a card, Table Talk makes an image on your device and hands it to the standard iOS share sheet. The destination you choose, such as Messages or a social app, handles it under its own privacy practices.

Usage analytics (PostHog)

When usage analytics is on, the app sends simple usage events to PostHog, our analytics provider. Examples are that a card session started or finished, or which screen you opened. Each event comes with a random ID created for this install, the app version, device model and iOS version. We don't create a profile of you, and we never send your name, email, contacts or anything you type. Like any internet service, PostHog receives your IP address when the app connects, and it may use that to estimate a rough location. Analytics is never used for advertising. When you turn the switch off, collection stops. See PostHog's privacy policy.

Crash reports (Sentry)

When crash reports are on, a crash or serious error sends a technical report to Sentry. It contains what went wrong in the code, the app version, device model and iOS version, so we can fix it. Crash reports don't include screenshots, your name or contact details, and Sentry's option for collecting personal information is turned off. See Sentry's privacy policy.

Weekly reminder

If you allow notifications, Table Talk schedules one local reminder a week (“Table Talk night”) with a conversation starter from the free deck. It's scheduled on your device, with no push server and no marketing. You can change the day and time or turn it off in Settings → Reminders.

Advertising

The free version shows banner ads on Home and during a card session. It may also show a full-screen ad when you leave a longer card session and return to Home, never in the middle of a card.

Ads are served through Google AdMob. For each ad, AdMob runs an auction in which Google and the partner ad networks listed below can bid. Not every partner is active at every moment. The networks, not Dudley Development, LLC, choose which ad you see. We don't receive your advertising identifier or any ad profile about you. We only see totals, such as how many ads were shown and what they earned.

Personalized ads happen only if you tap “Allow”

  • If you tap “Allow” on Apple's tracking prompt (App Tracking Transparency), ads may be personalized. Google and the partner networks may use your device's advertising identifier (IDFA) and information about your activity in other companies' apps and websites. They use it to pick ads that are more relevant to you and to measure how those ads perform. The app only reads the IDFA if you allow tracking.
  • If you tap “Ask App Not to Track”, turn tracking off later, or tracking is unavailable on your device, the app can't read the IDFA. Every ad request is marked non-personalized, and the app turns on Google's restricted data processing signal, which limits how Google and its partners may use the data. Non-personalized ads are chosen from context, such as the app you're using and your general region, not from a profile of you.
  • If the app can't tell which region your device is set to, it shows only non-personalized ads, even if you allowed tracking.
  • No ads in the EEA, the UK or Switzerland. If your device's region (iOS Settings → General → Language & Region) is a country in the European Economic Area (the 27 EU countries plus Iceland, Liechtenstein and Norway), the United Kingdom or Switzerland, Table Talk doesn't request ads at all. This uses the region setting, not your location.

Even non-personalized ads need some information to be delivered and counted. The networks may receive your IP address (which suggests your approximate location), your device model, iOS version, language, the app's name and version, and whether an ad was shown or tapped. They may also use an identifier that iOS limits to one developer's apps. They use this to show the ad, cap how often you see it, prevent fraud and report results. Table Talk never shares your precise location, contacts, photos or anything you type with ad networks.

Buying Remove Ads stops ads from showing.

How to change your choice

  • In the app, open Settings → Manage tracking. It takes you to Table Talk's page in iOS Settings, where you can turn Allow Tracking on or off.
  • Or go to iOS Settings → Privacy & Security → Tracking and turn Table Talk off. To stop every app from asking, turn off “Allow Apps to Request to Track”.

The change applies to every ad requested afterwards.

Your US state privacy rights

California residents have rights under the CCPA, as amended by the CPRA. Residents of Virginia, Colorado, Connecticut, Utah, Texas, Oregon and a growing number of other states with similar laws have comparable rights. This section explains how they apply to Table Talk.

“Selling”, “sharing” and targeted advertising

We don't sell personal information for money. However, if you allow tracking, the ad networks above receive your advertising identifier and ad-activity information so they can show personalized ads. California law calls this “sharing” for cross-context behavioral advertising, and it may also count as a “sale”. Other states call it “targeted advertising” or a “sale”. The categories involved are:

  • Identifiers: the advertising identifier (only if you allow tracking) and your IP address.
  • Internet or other electronic network activity: which ads were shown or tapped, and related technical data.
  • Approximate location: estimated from your IP address, never GPS.
  • Inferences: interests the ad networks may infer on their own.

We don't sell or share sensitive personal information. We don't knowingly sell or share personal information of anyone under 16.

How to opt out

For Table Talk, turning tracking off is how you opt out of the sale or sharing of your personal information and of targeted advertising. Use Settings → Manage tracking in the app, or iOS Settings → Privacy & Security → Tracking. After you opt out, the app no longer reads your advertising identifier, requests only non-personalized ads, and turns on the restricted data processing signal. You don't have to contact us or prove who you are. Apps don't see a browser's Global Privacy Control signal, so use the iOS setting.

Your other rights

Depending on where you live, you may have the right to know what personal information we have about you, and to access, correct, delete or get a copy of it. You also have the right not to be treated differently for using these rights. Table Talk has no accounts, and the little data we receive is tied to a random ID rather than to you, so usually we have nothing that identifies you. You can still ask: email support@dudleyapps.com with the subject “Privacy request”. We'll reply within the time your state law requires, usually 45 days. We may ask for details to find the data. An authorized agent can make a request for you, with written permission.

If we turn down your request, you can appeal by replying with the subject “Privacy appeal”. We'll explain our decision. If you're still unhappy, you can contact your state attorney general.

Purchases

Table Talk offers optional in-app purchases: Remove Ads, three prompt packs, a pack bundle and the Daily Featured Archive. Apple processes them. We never see or store your payment details, and Apple handles billing under Apple's privacy policy. Purchases can be restored on any device signed in to the same Apple Account from Settings → Restore Purchases.

How long data is kept

Data on your device stays until you delete the app. Analytics and crash data are kept only as long as they help us improve the app, then deleted under our PostHog and Sentry retention settings. We don't combine them with data from other sources or from our other apps.

Children

Table Talk is a general-audience app. Some packs are meant for families to play together, but the app isn't directed at children, and we don't knowingly collect personal information from children under 13. If you think a child has sent us personal information, email us and we'll delete it.
