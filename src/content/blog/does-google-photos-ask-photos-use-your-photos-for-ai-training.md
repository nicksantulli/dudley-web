Google says Gemini features in Google Photos, including Ask Photos, do not use your personal Photos data for ads and do not train generative AI models outside Google Photos with that personal Photos data. That is the short answer.

The practical answer is more nuanced: Ask Photos still processes your library so it can answer questions, suggest edits, identify memories, use face groups, and remember facts you explicitly give it. If you share a photo from Google Photos into another app or connect Google Photos to another Google service, that other service's policies may apply.

Sources: [Google Photos Gemini features privacy hub](https://support.google.com/photos/answer/15344015?hl=en), [Google: Ask button and Ask Photos tips](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/photos/ask-button-ask-photos-tips/), [Google Photos face groups help](https://support.google.com/photos/answer/6128838?hl=en), [Apple Developer: App privacy details](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/app-privacy-details/), and the current [VibeRater Social App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/viberater-social/id6780704282).

## The Short Answer

Ask Photos is a Gemini-powered feature inside Google Photos. It can search your library with natural-language questions, answer follow-ups about images, suggest related memories, and help with edits.

Google's current privacy hub says three important things for Gemini features in Photos:

- personal data in Google Photos is not used for ads;
- Google does not train generative AI models outside Google Photos with your personal data in Google Photos;
- human review of Ask Photos responses does not happen unless you give feedback or in rare cases involving abuse or harm.

That is reassuring, but it is not the same as "nothing is processed." To make Ask Photos work, Google Photos uses information from your library, face group labels, relationship labels, Google Account information, saved Remember List facts, and inferences about your photos.

## What Ask Photos Looks At

According to Google's privacy hub, Gemini features in Photos can use photos and videos in your library, face group labels and names, account information, relationship details you provide, and remembered facts you choose to save.

Google also says Photos may process images and videos to improve edits or make inferences. The examples Google gives include guessing the age and locations of top face groups and inferring life-related insights for features such as memories, edits, creations, and Ask Photos responses.

That means the privacy question is not simply "training or no training." The better question is: what does the feature need to look at, what does it remember, what is shared outside Google Photos, and what controls can you change later?

## The Big Caveat: Sharing and Connecting Services

Google draws a line around Gemini features inside Google Photos. The line can move when you connect Google Photos to another service or share a photo or video somewhere else.

For example, Google's privacy hub says if you share a photo from Google Photos with Gemini Apps or another third-party service, the shared photo is processed according to that other service's policies. It also says connected services may process and save information from Google Photos under their own policies.

That is the part people miss. A photo can begin in Google Photos, get surfaced by Ask Photos, then be shared to Messages, Gemini, Instagram, TikTok, a photo editor, or another social app. Each step can carry a different privacy expectation.

## What About Face Groups?

Ask Photos depends on Google Photos features such as face groups for some experiences. Google says face groups and labels are private to your account by default, and they are not shared when you share photos.

Google also says turning on face groups lets Google make models of faces in your photos, and those models may be considered biometric data in some jurisdictions. You can turn off face groups to delete face models, face groups, and face labels, subject to Google's retention language.

If you use Ask Photos, this is worth checking. A camera-roll AI assistant is more useful when it understands people, places, and relationships. That usefulness is exactly why the settings matter.

## How To Check Your Settings

Use this quick privacy review before asking AI to reason across your photo library:

1. Open Google Photos settings and review Gemini features in Photos.
2. Check whether Ask Photos is enabled for your account and region.
3. Review face groups, your "Me" face group, and any names or relationships you added.
4. Review the Remember List if you have saved facts for Ask Photos to use later.
5. Review Photos Activity and query donation settings.
6. Think twice before sharing a sensitive photo from Google Photos into another app.

The most sensitive images are usually not the obvious selfies. They are receipts, medical screenshots, school documents, home interiors, travel photos, private group photos, location-rich albums, and anything that reveals another person's life.

## What App Store Privacy Labels Can and Cannot Tell You

Apple's App Store privacy-details guidance tells developers to disclose data types they and their third-party partners collect, including user content such as photos or videos when an app uploads that content. Apple also distinguishes whether data is linked to the user and whether it is used for tracking.

Those labels are useful, but they are still summaries. They do not replace the app's privacy policy, the in-app permission screen, or the specific feature flow you are using.

For AI photo apps, check:

- whether the app needs one selected photo or broad library access;
- whether photos are processed only to service a request or retained afterward;
- whether generated or edited media can be posted, saved, or shared;
- whether the app uses third-party AI providers;
- whether face grouping, location, contacts, account data, or ad identifiers are involved;
- whether deleting a post, account, or activity history is supported.

## Where VibeRater Fits

[VibeRater Social](/apps/vibe-rater/) is not Google Photos, Ask Photos, Gemini, a photo-library search assistant, a biometric identity tool, or an AI training dashboard. It is also not affiliated with or endorsed by Google.

The useful connection is privacy context. A private camera roll, an AI-assisted search result, a playful rating, a saved share card, and a public social post are not the same thing.

VibeRater Social is built around entertainment and sharing controls. Its App Store listing describes ratings as creative expression, not biometric, health, identity, attractiveness, or clinical analysis. The product lesson is to keep the context visible: rate a moment, decide whether it stays private, share with a Squad when you mean to, or post only when the post belongs in public.

Dudley's [VibeRater privacy policy](/privacy/vibe-rater/) makes the same kind of distinction: rating-only photos are different from media you deliberately post, share, or save for social features.

## Quick Rule

If an AI feature can answer questions about your camera roll, it has to inspect some representation of your camera roll. That does not automatically mean ad targeting or broad model training, but it does mean settings, sharing paths, and saved memories matter.

Use Ask Photos for convenience. Use privacy settings for boundaries. Use sharing apps only when you are comfortable changing a private photo into something other people, platforms, or services may process.

## FAQ

### Does Google Photos Ask Photos use your photos for AI training?

Google says it does not train generative AI models outside Google Photos with your personal data in Google Photos. That is narrower than saying every photo-related AI use is impossible, because Gemini features in Photos still process your library to answer, edit, remember, and personalize inside Google Photos.

### Does Google use Google Photos data for ads?

Google says your personal data in Google Photos is never used for ads.

### Can humans review Ask Photos conversations?

Google says Ask Photos responses are not reviewed by humans unless you give feedback or in rare abuse or harm cases. Queries donated for review are separated from your account and do not include your photos, videos, Ask Photos response, or account information used to generate the response.

### What changes if I share a Google Photos image with Gemini or another app?

Google says the policy of the other Google service or third-party app applies when you share or connect Google Photos content to that service. Treat sharing as a new privacy decision, not as a continuation of the same Google Photos privacy boundary.

### Is VibeRater a Google Photos or Gemini alternative?

No. VibeRater Social is a social entertainment app for playful photo ratings, share cards, and chosen posts. It is not a camera-roll search assistant, a Google Photos feature, or a Gemini privacy tool.
