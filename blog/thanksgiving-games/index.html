Thanksgiving Table Games That Need Nothing but the People There

Written by Nicholas Santulli·Published November 3, 2026

Fill the stretch between turkey and pie with games that need no printing and no supplies: a no-repeat memory round, charades drawn from the room, one Would You Rather, and a hidden mission you assign out loud. Table Talk is a phone of prompts if you do not want to invent the next round.

Thanksgiving is November 26 this year. The turkey comes off the table, the pie is still a while off, and the room is full of people who are done eating and not ready to leave. That stretch is long enough for a game. It is not long enough for a supply run.

You can buy a printed pack for it. You can also play with the people already in the chairs. The rounds below need no printer, no purchase, and nothing you do not already have in the room.

Play one. If the table is already inside a story, leave it there.

A memory round, with no paper

Someone starts with a category the whole table can see. “Dishes on this table.” “People who cooked today.” “Places we came from.” The next person repeats the list and adds one thing. No repeats. A pass is allowed. When two people pass in a row, the round is over.

There is no score. The list is the game. A kid can add the rolls. A grandparent can add the dish nobody else remembered. If the category would sting, pick another one. Bodies, money, and politics are a bad category at this table. The meal in front of you is a good one.

This is the add-on game from long car rides: hear the list, then add one thing. It does not need a board.

Charades, drawn from the room

Charades needs a person willing to stand up and a table willing to guess. It does not need a printed list. The prompts are already in the room.

Name a pile out loud, then hand the turn to someone who wants it:

  • A dish on the table
  • A trip this family actually took
  • A chore that happens every year in this kitchen

The actor acts. The table shouts. Give it about a minute, then sit down. Passing the turn is part of the game. A person who does not want to perform should not have to.

Skip a point system unless someone asks for one. A scoreboard turns a short round into a contest the quiet end of the table did not enter.

One Would You Rather, not a stack

A forced choice is a different game from charades. Two options, a side, and a reason. One of them, while someone clears the plates, is enough. Ten of them is a quiz.

How to build a dilemma that can split the table, and when to skip it, is already in Why Would You Rather Works at the Dinner Table. Use a food trade-off or a seat. Leave politics out of the pair. If the room is already talking, you do not need the round.

Secret Mission, without the slip

Secret Mission is not our game. One way to play it puts a written task under each plate. The guest tries to finish the task, and the table reveals the tasks at the end. That way needs slips. You can skip the slips.

Before people sit, tell each person one mission out loud, where the others cannot hear. One sentence. They try to do it sometime before dessert. After the pie, go around: the table guesses, then the person says what the mission was.

Missions that fit a mixed-age Thanksgiving:

  • Find out who made one dish, and where the recipe came from.
  • Ask someone what they did this morning before they arrived.
  • Ask a kid what they are proud of, and save the answer for the reveal.
  • Learn the name of a side you have never asked about.

Missions that do not fit: a prank, a dare, a forced speech, or anything that makes a person the joke. If you cannot say the mission kindly, do not assign it.

You run it by talking to people in the kitchen. No paper.

When the stretch is not a game

Some tables do not need a round. They need the next question, or they need a fight parked before it starts. How to Keep Thanksgiving Dinner Conversation From Turning Into Politics (or Silence) is that job. A game will not do it. If someone is actually hurt, leave the game and step out of the room with them.

If the stretch ends with a separate phone in every hand, that is a screen problem, not a missing game. The Phone in the Middle of the Table is the note on one shared phone. This post stays with the rounds.

Frequently asked questions

What Thanksgiving table games need no supplies?

A memory round where each person adds one item and nobody repeats, charades drawn from the meal and the room, one Would You Rather, and a hidden-mission round you assign out loud instead of on paper. One round is enough for the stretch between turkey and pie.

How do you play Secret Mission at dinner without paper?

Secret Mission is not our game. One way to play it is a written slip under the plate. Skip the slip. Tell each guest one small, kind mission in private before they sit. They try to finish it before dessert. After pie, the table guesses, then each person says what the mission was. Skip pranks and anything that makes a person the joke.

What should you play between Thanksgiving dinner and dessert?

Play one short game the whole table can join or pass: a no-repeat list, a charades turn from the room, or a single Would You Rather. If people are already inside a story, leave them there. A political fight or a flat silence is a conversation problem, not a game.
