Does Visual Intelligence Send Photos to ChatGPT?

Written by Nicholas Santulli·Published August 24, 2026

Visual Intelligence can send a photo, screenshot, or onscreen item to ChatGPT only when you choose a ChatGPT action. Here is what Apple and OpenAI say is shared, saved, and controlled.

iPhone PrivacyAI Images & MediaApple IntelligenceVisual IntelligenceChatGPT

Visual Intelligence does not send your photos or screenshots to ChatGPT just because you open the feature. It can send visual content to ChatGPT when you choose a ChatGPT action, such as tapping Ask or asking Siri to use ChatGPT with a photo or file. Apple says you control when ChatGPT is used and that you are asked before photos or files are sent.

The practical rule is simple: Visual Intelligence can look at what is in front of you, on your screen, or in a screenshot. ChatGPT becomes part of that flow only when you choose a ChatGPT-powered request.

Sources: Apple Support: Use visual intelligence on iPhone, Apple Support: Use ChatGPT with Apple Intelligence, Apple: ChatGPT Extension & Privacy, OpenAI Help: How your data is handled when you use ChatGPT through Apple’s integrations, and OpenAI Help: Apple Intelligence - Visual Intelligence FAQ.

The Short Answer

Visual Intelligence can use different actions depending on what you are doing:

  • camera-based lookup for places, objects, plants, animals, text, and nearby context
  • onscreen or screenshot search for selected visual items
  • calendar, website, summarize, and read-aloud actions when available
  • ChatGPT questions when you choose the ChatGPT option

Apple’s Visual Intelligence guide says onscreen Visual Intelligence starts from the same button press you use for a screenshot, then lets you search, highlight an item, ask ChatGPT, add an event to Calendar, open a relevant website, summarize text, or read text aloud. Apple also says you can close the result and delete the screenshot or choose to save it.

That does not mean every screenshot is sent to ChatGPT. The ChatGPT step is a choice inside the flow.

What Gets Shared When You Use ChatGPT

Apple’s ChatGPT support page says that when you use the ChatGPT extension, your request and attachments are sent to ChatGPT along with limited context associated with the request, such as current time zone, country, device type, language, and the feature being used.

Apple also says your IP address is obscured from ChatGPT, while general location is provided for fraud prevention and legal compliance. If you use ChatGPT through Apple without signing in to a ChatGPT account, Apple says OpenAI does not receive information tied to your Apple Account.

OpenAI’s help page makes the account boundary important:

  • without a ChatGPT account connected, OpenAI says requests through Apple’s integrations are processed to provide a safe response and comply with law, not stored or used to train models
  • with a ChatGPT account connected, your ChatGPT account settings and OpenAI’s data privacy policies apply
  • if you continue a conversation in ChatGPT outside Apple’s integration, that later use follows ChatGPT’s normal app or web policies

So the same Visual Intelligence question can have different data handling depending on whether you are signed in to ChatGPT and where you continue the conversation.

Does Visual Intelligence Need Full Photos Access?

No. Visual Intelligence is not the same permission as giving a third-party app Full Access to your Photos library.

Visual Intelligence is an Apple system feature for things like camera lookup, onscreen search, and screenshot-based questions. Full Photos access is an app-level permission that can let an app work with your broader photo library.

If you are comparing privacy risk, separate these questions:

  • Did I use Apple’s system Visual Intelligence feature?
  • Did I tap a ChatGPT action?
  • Did I attach or show a photo, file, screen, or object?
  • Am I signed in to a ChatGPT account?
  • Did I give a separate app Full Access, Limited Access, or picker-only access to Photos?

Those are different controls. Do not treat them as one giant “AI can see my photos” switch.

How To Use It More Privately

If you want the narrower setup, use these habits:

  1. Keep the ChatGPT extension off unless you actually use it.
  2. Leave request confirmations on.
  3. Avoid connecting a ChatGPT account if you do not need account-based features.
  4. Ask about only the image, object, or screenshot you mean to analyze.
  5. Do not include private documents, faces, addresses, medical details, financial screens, or messages unless you are comfortable sending that content for the request.
  6. Close the Visual Intelligence result and delete the screenshot when you do not need to save it.

Apple says the ChatGPT extension is off by default and can be turned off later in Settings under Apple Intelligence & Siri. Apple also says photos and files require confirmation before being sent to ChatGPT.

What If Siri Suggests ChatGPT?

Siri can ask whether you want to use ChatGPT for some requests. Apple’s support page says Siri asks before using ChatGPT when it determines ChatGPT may be helpful, and Apple says you are always asked before photos or files are sent.

There is also a setting to send some Siri requests to ChatGPT without confirming, but Apple says photos and files still require confirmation. If you are privacy-sensitive, keep confirmations on and treat any photo or file prompt as a real upload decision.

How This Applies to Photo Apps

Visual Intelligence is a useful example of the broader photo-app rule: the important privacy moment is the moment you choose what to send.

The same applies to third-party photo, aura, meme, edit, and vibe apps. Before you upload, ask:

  • Does this feature need the whole photo library or only one selected item?
  • Is the result private, saved, posted, or shared?
  • Does the app explain what happens to selected media?
  • Are you using a system picker, Limited Access, or Full Access?
  • Does signing in change storage, history, personalization, or training settings?

That framing is more useful than panic-reading every AI feature as identical.
